## 1. Editorial Summary & Pitch Angles

### Pitch Angle 1: The Great American Naming Dispersion (1880–2024)
- **Headline**: *From "Mary" and "James" to 53,000+ Unique Names: How American Naming Diversity Quadrupled in a Century.*
- **Core Data Point**: In 1880, the top 10 male and female names accounted for over 35% of all American newborns. By 2024, the top 10 accounted for under 8% of births.
- **Resource URL**: https://howmanyofme.co/research/name-popularity-by-decade
- **Downloadable Dataset**: https://howmanyofme.co/data/us-historical-names-decade-summary.csv

### Pitch Angle 2: Actuarial Survival vs. Lifetime Births
- **Headline**: *Why You Can't Trust Lifetime Baby Name Stats: New Demographic Model Separates Living Population from Historical Births.*
- **Core Data Point**: Lifetime birth registrations overestimate living individuals by 35–40% due to historical mortality. Applying CDC/NCHS life tables produces true living population estimates.
- **Resource URL**: https://howmanyofme.co/methodology

## 2. Fast Facts for Press & Researchers

- **Most Registered Male Name in U.S. History**: **James** (4,712,453 births from 1880 to 2024).
- **Most Registered Female Name in U.S. History**: **Mary** (3,266,220 births from 1880 to 2024).
- **Longest #1 Run in American History**: **Mary** (Held the #1 spot for 66 consecutive years from 1880 through 1946).
